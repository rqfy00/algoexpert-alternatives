### NeetCode Pro: the video route

NeetCode's free tier carries the Blind 75 and NeetCode 150 roadmaps with video explanations, which is close to what people were paying AlgoExpert for four years ago. Pro adds company-tagged problems across 100+ companies, 200+ videos, 300+ practice problems in a browser editor, written guides and a private Discord.

Pricing checked in August 2026: $119 for one year (list $199), or $297 lifetime (list $599). Auto-renew is off by default, which is a small thing that saves a lot of people $119.

### LeetCode Premium: volume and company tags

The default answer, and the one with the biggest library. A public GraphQL query in August 2026 returned 4,029 problems. Premium is $35 per month or $159 per year, and buys company questions sortable by role, time and frequency, official solutions, the Ask Leet coding agent, a Lightning Judge and an interactive breakpoint debugger.

What it doesn't do is teach. There are patterns in the problem set, but nobody maps them for you. People rarely fail LeetCode rounds from lack of problems; they fail because they spent fifteen of forty-five minutes trying approaches that didn't fit.

### Educative: Grokking, in text

Home of the original "Grokking the Coding Interview" pattern courses, plus system design and object-oriented design tracks. Text-first with in-browser coding, which some people finish twice as fast as video. Pricing is subscription-based and has moved around a lot in recent comparisons (figures around $299/year get quoted), so check the current plan page before deciding.

### Design Gurus: the pattern course with system design attached

Founded by the authors of the original Grokking material, sold as individual courses and bundles. If system design is part of your loop, this is one of the few alternatives that covers both DSA patterns and system design without a second subscription. Course-level pricing, so the total depends on which bundle you pick.

### Paid mock interviews

Not a course, and not a replacement for one, but the highest-leverage money in most prep budgets. Platforms like interviewing.io pair you with working engineers for live sessions, billed per session rather than as a subscription. Rates vary by interviewer, so look at the current pricing page instead of trusting a number from a blog post.

### The free tier that covers more than people expect

Blind 75 and NeetCode 150 both exist free with explanations. LeetCode's free tier gives you the problem set minus a Premium-locked subset. freeCodeCamp and GeeksforGeeks cover fundamentals and reference material. If you're three weeks from starting, work the free path first and find out whether you're still at it before spending anything.

## Which one should you actually buy

**You have 4 to 8 weeks and you learn by reading.** AlgoMonster at $99. The entire pitch is a finishable curriculum with a defined endpoint, which is the opposite of staring at a 4,000-problem library.

**You want video explanations and you'll pay for the bundle.** AlgoExpert All Access. The content people bought in 2021 still gets spoken about well; the objection is that $119 now buys six products you probably don't need stapled to one you might.

**You need volume and company-specific questions.** LeetCode Premium at $159/year. This is the one case where paying more is straightforward, particularly if you're two weeks out from an onsite at a named company.

**You want video on a budget.** NeetCode. Its free tier is genuinely usable, and Pro is cheaper than AlgoExpert was before the bundle change.

**System design is a dedicated round in your loop.** None of the DSA-first platforms is enough on its own. Design Gurus, Educative's system design tracks, or a serious book, budgeted separately.

**You can already solve problems but freeze in the room.** Skip another course and buy three or four mock interviews. Pattern recognition is not the same skill as explaining a tradeoff while someone watches your cursor sit still.

The lifetime math is simple enough to do in your head: at $189 versus $99 a year, lifetime pays for itself if you're still using the material two years from now. For engineers who interview every two or three years by choice, that's an easy yes. If this is one job search and you'll be done in eight weeks, the yearly plan is the cheaper exit.

## What none of these platforms fix

Three gaps show up across the whole category, and AlgoMonster is not exempt from any of them.

**Nobody simulates real pressure.** Every practice problem is untimed, unwatched and uninterruptible. Real screens end with "how would this change if the input didn't fit in memory?" The timed assessments in AlgoExpert's bundle and Speedrun mode in AlgoMonster are approximations, not substitutes.

**System design coverage is thin wherever DSA is the main product.** AlgoMonster includes introductory system design and behavioral material. So does AlgoExpert through SystemsExpert. For L5+ loops, neither reaches the depth a dedicated round demands, and pretending otherwise is how people get caught out in week five.

**Text versus video is a real fork, not a preference.** If you know you retain better from watching someone solve a problem, no amount of good prose fixes that. If you read faster than you watch, video courses quietly cost you weeks.
